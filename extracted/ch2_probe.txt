       In Chapter 1 we talked a lot about the wave function and how you use it to calculate
       various quantities of interest. The time has come to stop procrastinating and confront
       what is, logically, the prior question: How do you get \JI (x, t) in the first place-how

       (the physicist's first line of attack on any partial differential equation): We look for
       solutions that are simple products,

       where lfr (lowercase) is a function of x alone, and f is a function of t alone. On its

       to be of great interest. Moreover, as is typically the case with separation of variables,
       we will be able at the end to patch together the separable solutions in such a way as
